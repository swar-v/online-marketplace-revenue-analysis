# Online Marketplace Revenue Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes product and customer revenue patterns for a synthetic online marketplace dataset containing 18,000 orders from 2022 through 2025.

SQL was used to aggregate, classify, and rank marketplace data. Python and pandas were used to validate the SQL results, while Matplotlib was used to visualize the key findings.

## Business Question

Which products and customer groups contribute the most net sales revenue, and how much revenue is generated by repeat customers compared with one-time customers?

## Initial Hypothesis

A relatively small group of products and repeat customers contributes a disproportionately large share of total net sales revenue.

## Dataset

The dataset contains 18,000 synthetic marketplace orders and 39 variables, including:

- Order and customer identifiers
- Product IDs and categories
- Order quantities and prices
- Discounts and net order values
- Customer, seller, delivery, and payment information

**Source:** [Online Marketplace Orders Dataset on Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/mmumairkhattak/online-marketplace-orders-dataset)

## Data Validation

The dataset was validated before analysis:

- 18,000 records and 39 columns
- 18,000 unique Order IDs
- No missing Order IDs
- No duplicate Order IDs
- No missing cells
- Order dates from January 1, 2022, through December 31, 2025
- No invalid financial ranges
- Discount differences were limited to immaterial rounding differences of $0.01 or less

A product master-data issue was also identified: 3,797 of the 4,143 Product IDs, approximately 91.65%, were associated with multiple product categories. Product-level analysis therefore used Product ID alone, while category analysis was performed separately at the order level.

## Key Findings

### Marketplace Overview

- Total orders: 18,000
- Unique customers: 6,068
- Unique products: 4,143
- Total net revenue: $2,612,304.41
- Average order value: $145.13

### Customer Findings

- Repeat customers generated 94.28% of total net revenue.
- Repeat customers placed 16,930 of the 18,000 orders.
- Repeat-customer average order value was $145.48.
- One-time-customer average order value was $139.63.
- The highest-revenue 10% of customers generated 28.69% of total revenue.
- Of the 607 customers in the highest-revenue 10%, 603 were repeat customers.

### Product Findings

- The highest-revenue 10% included 415 of the 4,143 products.
- These products generated $653,961.46.
- They contributed 25.03% of total net revenue.

### Product-Category Findings

- Fashion generated the highest category revenue at $427,214.22.
- Electronics ranked second in revenue and recorded the highest average order value at $152.04.
- Fashion, Electronics, Home & Kitchen, and Beauty generated a combined 55.78% of total revenue.

## Hypothesis Evaluation

The product portion of the hypothesis was supported. The highest-revenue 10% of products generated 25.03% of total net revenue, approximately 2.5 times the share expected under an even revenue distribution.

The repeat-customer portion was also supported, with an important qualification. Repeat customers generated 94.28% of total revenue, but they represented 82.37% of customers and were therefore not a small customer group.

Overall, repeat purchasing and higher-performing products were important revenue drivers in this dataset.

## Business Recommendations

1. Prioritize customer-retention initiatives because repeat customers generated 94.28% of revenue.
2. Develop loyalty and personalized promotion strategies for high-value repeat customers.
3. Encourage one-time customers to make a second purchase through follow-up offers and product recommendations.
4. Maintain availability and visibility for the highest-revenue products.
5. Give particular attention to Fashion and Electronics.
6. Improve product master-data quality before making detailed product-category decisions.

## Project Limitations

- The dataset is synthetic and does not represent verified marketplace behavior.
- Product ID–category relationships were inconsistent.
- Revenue was analyzed rather than profit because sufficient product-cost information was unavailable.
- Customer classification was limited to the 2022–2025 observation period.
- The results identify associations but do not establish causation.
